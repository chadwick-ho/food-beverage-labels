PET Label Coverage: Define the Numerator Before Quoting a Percentage

Recognition of one adhesive is treated as evidence for a different printed and finished label system.

Updated: 31 October 2026 | English B2B packaging purchasing guide.

Editorial purchasing guidance. Packaging examples are illustrative.

A coverage percentage is incomplete without its numerator and reference area. Identify every label component and ask the responsible assessor how the actual package must be measured.

Compare the tested or recognized printed construction with the complete proposed label, then refer differences for review. This avoids treating one matching component code as proof of the entire bottle's recycling outcome.

Keep component area separate from a coverage conclusion

List every external label component on the proposed bottle: front, back, neck, closure strip or other actual piece. Preserve its cut drawing and location. A calculation based only on the main brand label can omit material that the assessor needs to review.

Ask the responsible assessment provider which reference area and measurement method apply to its current guidance. Do not choose a convenient bottle photograph, flat rectangle or projected silhouette and call it the official denominator. A tapered or shaped bottle can require a different treatment from a simple developed panel.

Two different quantities can be useful in a drawing review. Summing the component areas describes the total cut material area. Counting the area covered at least once describes a surface union, which subtracts overlapping regions. Neither automatically equals the quantity required by a recycling assessment. Preserve both meanings where they are relevant rather than substitute one without disclosure.

In a purely geometric illustration, two regions have areas 40 and 30 square units, with 5 square units overlapping. Their summed material area is 70; their union is 65. Against a fictional reference area of 100, those display as 70 percent and 65 percent. This is set arithmetic, not a recycling rule, a bottle measurement or a pass threshold.

An overlap also places more than one material layer in that region. Describing its projected union does not erase that construction. Supply the complete face, adhesive, print and finish information alongside the geometry so the reviewer can use the required basis.

If the applicable method is unknown, submit the drawings and keep the percentage unresolved. Do not print a recyclability claim from this worksheet. Its purpose is to make the input question precise before comparing evidence scope below; package compatibility and actual local collection remain separate decisions requiring their own support.

Request the scope, not just a logo

Ask for the applicable report, recognition or guidance reference and its date/version. Identify the submitted construction and the limitations stated by its issuer. A cropped badge or a sales slide cannot show all of that scope.

Use historical recognition accurately

UPM's October2021 printed-PP recognition announcement identifies a particular printed label/adhesive/ink-and-varnish combination. It is an example of construction-specific evidence, not a current approval of every label using a similar polymer or adhesive.

Keep the historical date visible when using an older reference. Obtain current applicable documentation for the actual offer rather than calling the old announcement a fresh validation of your design.

Keep three outcomes separate

Use-phase adhesion and appearance answer whether the bottle label performs before disposal. Recycling-process compatibility concerns the defined material stream and assessment. Actual collection and processing depend on the market and available system. A positive result in one area should not be restated as success in all three.

If the design changes a metalized layer, coating, bottle color or coverage, identify the change before reusing the prior conclusion. The responsible reviewer decides its significance; the buyer should not infer a new result from the artwork preview.

For returnable-glass washing, use the separate reuse brief. For wet-use label evidence, use sample coverage. For protective finish scope, identify the actual top layer before the recycling review.

Short answer

A wash-off label is designed to hold during the product's use phase and release under defined recycling wash conditions. The goal is to let the label and adhesive separate from PET flakes so cleaner bottle material can continue through the recycling process.

The phrase sounds simple. The buying decision is not. A label face, adhesive, ink, coating, bottle color and coverage area all enter the package assessment. Local infrastructure matters too. A supplier cannot turn a global sustainability promise into fact with one material name.

What wash-off technology is trying to solve

During use, a beverage label has to survive filling, condensation, transport and the customer's hand. During recycling, that same bond may need to release in a controlled wash so the label material can separate from PET. Good performance therefore looks contradictory: stay on now, come off later.

A typical launch brief: "make it sustainable"

Consider a composite inquiry. A beverage team asks for a clear label on a clear PET bottle and wants a recycling symbol added. The intention is good. The brief does not state label coverage, adhesive code, ink coverage or destination market.

In this hypothetical inquiry, the relevant construction and intended market need evidence before a packaging claim is approved.

Five parts of the package to review

  • Bottle: PET resin, color and any barrier construction.
  • Label face: paper or film type, density and coverage.
  • Adhesive: use-phase bond plus release under specified wash conditions.
  • Printing and finish: ink, varnish, lamination and metallic decoration.
  • Market: collection, sorting and recycling guidance where the package is sold.

Construction-specific supplier evidence should be checked for the exact proposed bottle and label system.

Where a standard label can still be the honest choice

A project without applicable compatibility evidence should keep that conclusion unresolved. Do not describe a general waterproof stock as recycling-compatible merely because it is a practical launch option.

What to request from a label supplier

  • Face material and adhesive product codes.
  • Intended bottle resin and compatible recycling stream.
  • Test method, guidance reference or third-party compatibility evidence.
  • Limitations for ink, coverage, lamination and bottle color.
  • Use-phase testing for condensation and handling.
  • Written language that supports any proposed packaging claim.

For clear bottle artwork, read Clear Labels and White Ink. For moisture performance, use the Waterproof Beverage Label Guide. Confirm final environmental claims with the responsible brand and compliance team for the destination market.
