Beverage Multipack Labels: Approve the Pack-Level Scan

Plan beverage multipack barcode labeling with a finished-pack scan walkthrough. Confirm selling level, visible unit codes, print evidence and receiving behavior.

Updated: 25 October 2026 | English B2B packaging purchasing guide.

Custom Label Factory editorial guidance. Scenarios and packaging artwork are illustrative; no customer order, test result or regulatory approval is claimed.

Run a pack-level scan walkthrough before release

A code-quality report and a correct product identity should meet at the finished pack. Agree the intended sale level with the brand and receiving partner, then walk an assembled sample through that specific handling route. The following record helps keep the results attached to one pack revision.

Checkpoint Record on the assembled sample Decision owner
Selling unit Single can, retail multipack or another agreed level Brand/data owner
Approved identity Authorized identifier for that level and flavor/count Brand/data owner
Exposed scan views Pack faces presented during normal handling; any visible inner symbols Packaging engineer
Print quality Applicable verification evidence for the printed symbol Agreed verification party
Receiving behavior What the partner's system identifies when that pack is scanned Retail/distribution partner
Release boundary Sample, carrier artwork and assembly revision represented Packer and buyer

Do not assemble the report from a loose carrier proof, a different flavor's can and an old system screenshot. Those objects may each be correct while failing to establish that this finished multipack is correct. Identify substitutions and leave their effects open until the relevant owner accepts them.

GS1 Sweden's placement guidance addresses hiding symbols on inner consumer units in a composite pack. Use the applicable current specification and receiving requirements for the actual job. Do not solve an unwanted inner-code exposure by deleting or altering a unit identifier that the brand needs for individual sale. Change the agreed pack arrangement or carrier design and review the assembled result.

For a hypothetical mixed-flavor promotion, the stock carrier may fit the cans perfectly but identify the wrong assortment. The buyer should obtain the authorized pack identity before ordering the promotional labels, then include that carrier in the assembly clearance instructions. This example does not establish an identifier or claim any retailer has accepted the pack.

After a carrier, count or artwork change, identify which previous checks still apply and which need repeating. Retain the accepted assembled reference and the receiving partner's decision with the production file. A scan that returns a number is useful evidence only when the responsible owner also confirms what that number means for this selling unit.

A four-pack is more than four attractive cans

When a beverage buyer asks for a carrier sticker, I ask whether the retailer sells the cans individually, the four-pack, or both. The answer controls which barcode belongs on which level and what the checkout scanner must see. The pack should not look like a random enlarged unit label.

GS1 General Specifications define trade-item identification and barcode rules. The brand must assign and validate its own identifiers for its markets. The rendered code in this article's fictional illustration is decorative; it is not usable retail data.

Two beverage launches with different risks

Imagine a composite local soda brand that sells a four-pack only. It can design the paperboard carrier as the obvious checkout surface and place the pack barcode where the retailer expects it. I would still check that any can barcode visible through openings cannot be scanned accidentally during pack checkout.

A second composite brand sells both single cans and four-packs. It needs a controlled hierarchy: unit identifiers on cans, a separate pack identifier on the carrier, and a line process that applies the correct carrier to the correct flavor. The expensive mistake is not a crooked sticker; it is a beautiful four-pack with the wrong product identity.

Draw the scan view before the artwork proof

Build one packed sample. Turn it as a cashier, warehouse picker and shelf shopper might. Mark visible can codes, carrier folds, glue areas and the flat surface available for the pack symbol. Put the pack information on a stable panel with enough clear space for the final barcode artwork. Verify the symbol after printing and after the carrier is assembled.

LevelWhat to confirmWho signs off
Individual canUnit identity and required can informationBrand and compliance owner
Four-pack carrierDistinct pack identity and readable barcodeBrand, retailer or distributor

Prove both appearance and identity

Ask for a production-like carrier with final stock, ink and adhesive. Check the barcode zone after folding and after cartons are packed. GS1's barcode guide covers symbol choice, placement and print quality; the relevant retailer may add its own acceptance conditions. Do not approve a code merely because a phone camera reads it once.

Our buying view

Identity is a packaging feature

I would settle the pack hierarchy before polishing carrier graphics. A sticker can carry beautiful citrus artwork and still fail at checkout. The right purchase is the printed construction plus a pack-level proof that the brand and retailer actually approve.

Put these items in the multipack brief

  • Single-can and pack selling units with approved identifiers.
  • Physical cans and fully assembled carrier samples.
  • Final barcode artwork supplied by the brand, not invented by the printer.
  • Carrier material, application sequence and fold locations.
  • Retailer scan or verification acceptance criteria.

For code-quality decisions, read barcode verification versus scanning. Our barcode and QR label guide covers layout planning on other packs.

Frequently asked questions

Does a four-pack need its own identifier?

When the pack is a distinct trade item, GS1 identification rules generally require its own GTIN. Confirm the selling hierarchy and market with your GS1 organization or retail partner.

Can the unit barcode remain visible through the carrier?

That can create a checkout risk when the pack is sold as one unit. Test the finished carrier and retailer scanning process.

Is a phone scan enough to approve the artwork?

No. Use the relevant barcode specification and verification process for the intended retail or distribution environment.
