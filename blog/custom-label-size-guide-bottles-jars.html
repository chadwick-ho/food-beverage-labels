Packaging Fit Guide

Custom Label Size Guide for Bottles, Jars, Cups and Pouches

The best label size is not the biggest size. It is the size that fits the real package, leaves readable copy space and applies cleanly.

Last updated: September 9, 2026 | Best for: bottle labels, jar labels, food labels, beverage labels, cup labels, can labels and pouch labels.

Short answer

Choose a custom label size by measuring the usable flat or gently curved area of the package, then matching that space to the information buyers need to read. Avoid forcing artwork onto sharp curves, seams, ridges, caps or squeeze zones.

Practical rule: send a clear package photo with a ruler or marked label area. A real container photo prevents many size mistakes before proofing.

Label size planning by package type

PackageCommon label choiceSize issue to watch
Glass bottleFront label, back label or wraparound label.Shoulder curve and condensation can affect edge lift.
Food jarFront label, lid label, seal strip or matched set.Jar taper and lid radius can make large labels wrinkle.
Plastic cupClear film label, front label or cup transfer label.Cup taper changes how a rectangle sits on the surface.
CanFull wrap, partial wrap or front label.Measure straight wall height and avoid rim areas.
Stand-up pouchFront panel label or front/back set.Leave space away from gussets, zipper lines and heat seals.

How to measure a bottle or jar label

  • Measure the height of the straight label panel, not the full bottle height.
  • Wrap a strip of paper around the container to estimate label width.
  • Leave a small gap if the label wraps around instead of overlapping too tightly.
  • Check whether the label crosses a curve, seam, texture, rib or molded logo.
  • Apply a paper mockup first if the label shape is large, tall or custom die-cut.

Front label, back label or wraparound?

A front label is easier for branding and display. A back label is useful for ingredients, barcode, nutrition area, storage text or importer details. A wraparound label gives more visual coverage, but it needs better measurement and application control.

Do not choose size before copy

Small labels can look premium, but they may not leave enough room for readable ingredients, flavor name, net quantity, barcode or multilingual text. Before printing, list the required copy and check whether the font can remain readable after the label is scaled to the package.

What to send for a label size review

FAQ

Can I use the same label size on several jars?

Sometimes yes, but jar diameter, taper and panel height must be checked. A size that fits one jar may wrinkle or feel oversized on another.

Should I make my label as large as possible?

Usually no. A slightly smaller label that avoids curves and seams often looks cleaner and applies better.

Can the factory help adjust label size?

Yes. Send package photos, measurements and artwork, then the proof can be reviewed before bulk production.
