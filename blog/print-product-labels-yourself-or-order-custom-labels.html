Print Labels Yourself or Outsource: Decide Which Work You Keep

A founder compares printer cost with a supplier price while leaving artwork, coding, inspection and rework unassigned.

Updated: 21 October 2026 | English B2B packaging buyer guide.

Editorial purchasing guidance. Packaging examples are illustrative.

Buying printed labels does not transfer ownership of product copy, SKU data or the packing operation. Printing in-house does not remove the need to control those tasks. A useful make-or-buy decision starts by naming the work the business actually wants to move.

Draw the responsibility boundary before comparing a desktop printer with a custom-label quotation. The right answer can be a controlled split: outsourced stable graphics and in-house batch coding, where the actual construction and equipment support it.

List the work before assigning its cost

Use the same product, packing task and acceptance requirement for both options. Identify who performs each activity, how often it recurs and which evidence completes it. A printer purchase does not provide a trained operator or approved artwork by itself.

Put unknown entries in the sheet rather than treating them as zero cost. If a team does not yet know its reject or troubleshooting workload, run a controlled representative task and record it. Use accepted finished packs as the comparison basis when practical; an unused pile of attractive labels is not the final business output.

Separate flexibility from uncontrolled variation

In-house printing can be useful when a small team needs frequent legitimate revisions. It also makes it easy to print a file saved on the wrong laptop. Name the approved source file, authorized operator and obsolete-file handling in either workflow.

Outsourcing can reduce internal printing work, but a large delivered inventory may constrain later changes. Record which parts of the information are stable and which arise at packing. A proposed blank coding area should be evaluated with the actual printer, ribbon or other marking method and final label surface. Do not assume a glossy purchased label will accept the team's later code.

Compare the next ordinary month and one awkward month

Estimate the work in a normal month, then consider a realistic exception such as a copy correction or a short reorder. Use the business's own quantities and costs, with assumptions visible. Include only relevant incremental equipment costs; do not count the full purchase twice through both a capital line and a separate depreciation allocation.

If the team already owns a suitable printer, its past purchase is different from the future cash needed to continue using it. Capacity, maintenance and operator workload still matter. If a new purchase is proposed, confirm media compatibility and a meaningful sample before treating advertised resolution as finished-pack quality.

Short answer

Print product labels yourself when you are testing a few units, changing copy every week, or using simple black text labels that do not need premium shelf presentation. Order custom labels when the label must look retail-ready, match color consistently, survive moisture or oil, use a custom shape, or run smoothly on a packaging line.

Choose DIY printing when flexibility is worth more than finish

  • You need a small number of labels for a market test, photo shoot or sample handout.
  • The product name, ingredients, flavor or warning copy changes constantly.
  • The label is mostly black text and does not need photo-quality color.
  • The package is dry, flat and not exposed to refrigeration, water, ice, oil or heavy handling.
  • You are using the printout to test size and layout before ordering final labels.

Choose factory labels when the label is part of the product

For food, beverage and wine packaging, the label is often the first quality signal a buyer sees. Factory production becomes more sensible when the label must carry brand color, survive handling, wrap a curved surface, include small readable text, scan as a barcode, or match a future re-order.

Cost checklist before buying a label printer

Many founders compare only the price of blank labels against printed labels. That misses the real cost. Add printer cost, ink or toner, wasted sheets, failed media, trimming time, color troubleshooting, laminate, replacement parts and the value of your own production time.

A simple way to decide: estimate how many labels you need per month, how many designs change per month, whether the label must be waterproof, and how much time you can spend printing. If volume grows and design stabilizes, custom labels usually become the cleaner path.

What to send when you are ready for a custom label quote

  • Packaging photos, including one straight-on image and one angled image.
  • Exact label width and height, or the available label area on the package.
  • Quantity for each design, such as 100 pcs, 500 pcs, 1,000 pcs or 10,000 pcs.
  • Storage and use conditions: dry shelf, refrigerated, frozen, wet bottle, oily jar or other stated handling.
  • Application method: by hand, label dispenser, semi-auto labeler or production machine.
  • Artwork file and any required barcode, QR code, ingredient panel, nutrition panel or warning text area.
