Label Pricing Guide

How Much Do Custom Product Labels Cost?

A practical guide to the seven specifications that shape label pricing, the costs buyers often overlook, and the information that makes a quote accurate.

Last updated: September 18, 2026 | Best for: food, beverage and wine brands comparing custom label printing quotes.

Written and reviewed by the Custom Label Factory production team. Based on packaging specifications, artwork checks and label production planning. Final pricing depends on the confirmed project specification.

Short answer

Custom product label cost depends on the specification, not the product category alone. Label size, quantity per design, material, adhesive, finish, shape and roll or sheet format all affect the quote. Shipping, tooling and proof requirements can also change the final amount.

If you only ask, “How much are 1,000 labels?” the answer may look precise but still be misleading. One thousand small paper rectangles for dry boxes are a different production job from 1,000 waterproof die-cut film labels for chilled bottles.

If you remember one thing: the cheapest quoted label is not always the lowest-cost label. Compare the landed cost of labels that are actually usable on your package.

The seven factors that change custom label pricing

For many standard projects, the two most important inputs are label size and quantity per artwork. Material becomes equally important when the package is wet, cold, oily, flexible or difficult to bond. This is why a quote form should ask about the package environment before offering a material.

Total quantity and quantity per SKU are not the same

Suppose a brand needs 1,500 labels. If all 1,500 use one design, the job has one artwork version. If the order contains three flavors with 500 labels each, production must control three files, three proofs and three finished inventories. The combined quantity is identical, but the work is not.

This distinction matters most for brands with seasonal flavors or products that sell at different speeds. Equal quantities may create excess stock for the slowest SKU. The multi-SKU label printing guide explains how to plan quantities by product velocity instead of dividing the order evenly.

How to compare label quotes without fooling yourself

Put every supplier quote into the same comparison table. Do not compare a factory price that excludes freight with a delivered price that includes it. Do not compare a standard paper label with a laminated waterproof film label simply because both lines say “1,000 pieces.”

A useful comparison shortcut

Calculate cost per usable label

All-in landed amount ÷ labels you can actually use = cost per usable label.

Include printing, tooling, proof samples, freight and other project charges in the numerator. Reduce the denominator for labels that do not fit, cannot survive the package environment, arrive in the wrong roll direction or become obsolete before use. This simple calculation turns a cheap-looking quote into a more realistic business comparison.

Where to reduce cost, and where not to

If the quote is over budget, change one variable at a time so you can see what actually saves money. Start with options that simplify production without weakening label performance:

  • Use one shared size and material across related SKUs.
  • Choose a standard rectangle, circle or rounded-corner shape.
  • Remove decorative finishes that do not support the product position.
  • Provide clean, print-ready artwork and an accurate quantity for each version.
  • Consolidate shipping when the launch schedule allows it.

Do not save money by selecting an adhesive that has not been reviewed for the package, shrinking required copy until it is difficult to read, or skipping a fit test on a tapered, chilled or oily container. Those choices reduce the quote by moving cost into rework, relabeling or unusable inventory.

Our view: buy the lowest-risk specification

We do not think every product needs the most expensive label construction. We do think the specification should survive the real package and packing process. A dry box does not need the same material as a bottle covered in condensation, and a 100-piece launch test should not carry the same inventory commitment as a stable repeat order.

The best quote is the simplest specification that performs reliably. “Simplest” matters because unnecessary finishes consume budget. “Performs reliably” matters because a label that peels, wrinkles or cannot run on the applicator is expensive at any unit price.

What to send for an accurate custom label quote

  • Label width and height, plus the desired shape.
  • Quantity for each artwork or SKU.
  • Photos of the actual bottle, jar, pouch, cup, can or box.
  • Package material and whether the surface is flat, curved or tapered.
  • Exposure to water, condensation, refrigeration, freezing, oil or abrasion.
  • Paper, white film, clear film or undecided material preference.
  • Matte, gloss, foil or other finish requirements.
  • Hand, dispenser or machine application, including roll specifications when known.
  • Artwork files and target delivery destination.

If some details are unknown, send the package photo and describe the storage and application process. A useful supplier should help identify the missing specification rather than turning an assumption into a production problem. Frequently asked questions

How much do 100 custom product labels cost?

There is no reliable universal price for 100 labels. Size, material, shape, finish, artwork count, packing format and shipping all affect the quote. A complete specification is more useful than a generic per-piece estimate.

Are roll labels cheaper than sheet labels?

Not automatically. Roll labels can be efficient for repeated production and applicators, while sheets can suit samples or careful hand application. Compare the format that fits your workflow, not only the printed unit price.

Why do multiple label designs cost more?

Each artwork version needs separate proofing, file control and production planning. Three designs with 500 labels each are not always equivalent to one design with 1,500 labels, even when the total quantity is the same.

Do custom-shaped labels cost more?

They can. A custom dieline may add tooling or setup cost, and complex shapes may create more waste or slower finishing. A standard rectangle, circle or rounded corner shape is often the simpler budget option.
