A Label Quality Complaint: Preserve the Pack and Roll Before Rework

Prepare evidence that lets supplier and buyer investigate the same failure. Use a practical buyer checklist to define inputs, evidence, responsibilities and re

Published: 24 October 2026 | English B2B packaging buyer guide.

By Custom Label Factory. Editorial purchasing guidance; packaging examples are illustrative.

A sauce jar arrives with a lifted corner, so someone presses it down, wipes the surface and removes another label to inspect the glue. The team is trying to help, but the original condition has changed before the supplier sees it.

Preserve the observed pack, roll identity and process history before rework where the responsible team can do so appropriately. A useful complaint brief distinguishes the symptom, affected stock and evidence from the proposed cause. That gives buyer and supplier a shared starting point.

Describe what was observed

Record the location, timing and visible condition. Was the edge lifted before packing, after chilling or after transport? Did the printed surface smear, the label move or the web break? Those are observations. Weak adhesive, poor curing or bad winding are possible explanations that need relevant evidence.

The bottle-bubble investigation guide locates the first changed checkpoint. Apply that discipline to the complaint brief: state the earliest supported observation and mark earlier stages unknown where records are missing.

Use photographs that show the whole package and the affected detail. Preserve identification and scale where relevant, while avoiding a single close-up that makes it impossible to identify the container or label position.

Protect the material's history

Record who collected the sample, where it came from and what handling occurred after the issue was noticed. Keep a returned retail pack separate from an untouched production retain. If somebody already cleaned or peeled the sample, record that action rather than describe it as received condition.

Ask the responsible quality or technical owner how samples should be stored, transported or examined. Food-containing packs may need particular handling. This article does not authorize unsafe retention, reopening or testing of product.

For sticky edges, the adhesive-ooze guide emphasizes preserving contact surfaces. Cleaning can remove useful evidence, even when it temporarily makes the roll easier to handle.

Build a complaint evidence pack

Record Information to preserve
Product and label SKU, artwork revision, construction and component
Supply identity Supplier, order, lot, roll and carton references
Observed condition Symptom, location, first known time and photographs
Process Container, application route and relevant settings/conditions
Exposure Storage, handling, packing and transport history known so far
Samples Affected material, comparison retain and their histories
Extent Quantity and locations confirmed affected versus still uncertain
Containment Material held and authorized next action
Questions Evidence gaps and proposed investigation owners

The pack is an editorial investigation aid. It does not establish root cause, liability or the appropriate product disposition.

Separate containment from diagnosis

The business may need to hold material before the cause is known. Record that precautionary boundary and the person authorizing it. Do not present the hold quantity as the confirmed defective quantity if the extent is still being assessed.

Identify other rolls, containers or finished runs that may share relevant characteristics. Preserve the reasoning for inclusion or exclusion. A shared supplier name alone is weak evidence that every delivery is affected, while a single acceptable sample does not clear unidentified related stock.

The peeling guide describes several conditions to review. Use it to organize questions rather than select a cause from a menu and announce it before examining the actual process.

Agree the next observation before consuming samples

Ask buyer and supplier which questions the available samples can answer and which checks require additional material. Keep unused samples available where practical. If a destructive examination is proposed, identify the sample and record the condition before the action.

Use a relevant comparison. An affected production bottle and a clean office sample may differ in several ways. A useful investigation preserves those differences and chooses controlled follow-up work rather than assumes the adhesive alone changed.

Do not invent test thresholds or numerical tolerances after seeing the result. Use the agreed specification and appropriate methods. If the acceptance criterion is missing, resolve that gap explicitly with the responsible parties.

Keep operational needs visible

The production planner needs to know what stock is available, held or awaiting a decision. The supplier needs to know the affected material and evidence. Combining those questions in one urgent request to replace everything can make both harder to resolve.

Ask for the actual proposed containment, replacement, rework or investigation arrangement and its timing. Do not promise a free replacement or guaranteed restart date on behalf of a supplier without confirmation.

For a hypothetical chutney run, a limited group of rolls might be held while the team checks the application records. That boundary can change as evidence arrives. Record each change and its basis so the final extent is traceable.

Separate the commercial response from the technical finding

A supplier may agree an urgent replacement while the investigation remains open. Record that commercial action without treating it as an admission of a specific cause. Likewise, a technical observation does not automatically determine compensation. Keeping those decisions distinct lets operations recover while preserving an accurate account of what the evidence actually established.

Close the complaint with more than a new roll

Record the supported conclusion, remaining uncertainty, agreed disposition and any corrective action. If a new material is proposed, qualify the change rather than treat replacement delivery as proof that the original cause has been established.

Update the relevant specification or process instruction only when the evidence supports the change. Keep the complaint record linked to the affected lot and later trial results. A resolved commercial issue and a completed technical investigation may reach completion at different times.

Send a label-complaint brief with the observed symptom, roll and package identities, relevant process history and untouched sample availability. State what is held and what remains uncertain.
